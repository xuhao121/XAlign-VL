\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate three systems on the Multi30K test split:

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{Trained (ours).}] Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned with LoRA and our
cross-lingual consistency loss
$\mathcal{L}\!=\!\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}\!+\!\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}}$.
\item[\textbf{Nontrained.}] The off-the-shelf Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline,
prompted in each target language to generate that language natively.
\item[\textbf{Nontrained\,+\,MT.}] The same baseline, but used only to
generate English; the German, French and Czech captions are obtained by
machine-translating the English output with Helsinki-NLP MarianMT.
\end{description}

Each system produces one caption per (image, language) pair on the
$N_{\text{img}}\!=\!5{,}800$-image test set (the standard Multi30K
1000-image test enriched with the official 5-reference extension), for
a total of $4 N_{\text{img}}\!=\!23{,}200$ generations per system.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We follow a two-track design that avoids any translate-then-compare
step at evaluation time.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Generation quality:} BERTScore F1 between
$\text{pred}(L)$ and $\text{ref}(L)$ in the \emph{same} language $L$,
using \texttt{bert-base-multilingual-cased}. Higher is better.
\item \textbf{Cross-lingual consistency:} cosine similarity between
$\text{embed}(\text{pred}(L_1))$ and $\text{embed}(\text{pred}(L_2))$
for the same image, using the multilingual sentence encoder
\texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}. Per image this gives
$\binom{4}{2}\!=\!6$ pairwise scores. Higher is better.
\end{enumerate}

Significance for paired image-level comparisons is reported with a
one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($H_1$: Trained $>$ baseline).
All BERTScore and cosine values below are multiplied by $100$ for
readability.

\subsection{Generation quality}

Table~\ref{tab:bertscore} reports BERTScore F1.

Trained obtains the highest BERTScore in every language and in the
overall mean. The absolute gain over the native-generation baseline is
$+7.07$ on Czech, $+4.66$ on German, $+3.34$ on French and $+1.10$ on
English, ordered inversely to each language's baseline score
(CS $<$ DE $<$ FR $<$ EN).

At the image level, Trained scores strictly higher than Nontrained on
$90.7\%$ of Czech images, $83.0\%$ of German, $76.6\%$ of French and
$57.6\%$ of English. The one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test rejects
the null at $p<10^{-80}$ for every language.

Against the translation-pipeline baseline, Trained remains best in
every language (overall $\Delta\!=\!+1.22$). Per-language image-level
win rates range from $57.5\%$ (EN) to $62.4\%$ (FR), and the
difference is significant at $p<10^{-49}$ in every language.

\subsection{Cross-lingual consistency}

Tables~\ref{tab:consistency} and~\ref{tab:anchored} report pairwise
and per-language anchored consistency.

Trained raises the mean pairwise cosine by $+5.87$ over Nontrained,
with positive gains on all six language pairs. The gains on the three
EN--$L$ pairs ($+7.79$, $+7.30$, $+9.00$) are larger than those on the
three non-English pairs ($+3.11$, $+4.16$, $+3.85$).

In the per-language view (Table~\ref{tab:anchored}), English is the
lowest-anchored language under Nontrained ($68.15$) and the highest
under Trained ($76.18$, $\Delta\!=\!+8.03$). The cross-language spread
narrows from a $3.30$-point range $[68.15, 71.45]$ to a $1.74$-point
range $[74.73, 76.47]$.

Nontrained\,+\,MT obtains the highest raw cosine ($90.42$ overall),
which reflects the determinism of the shared English source under
MarianMT translation rather than independent cross-lingual generation
(Table~\ref{tab:consistency} caption).

\subsection{Per-image correlation of quality and consistency}

Table~\ref{tab:corr} reports the Pearson correlation between per-image
mean BERTScore and per-image mean pairwise cosine. The two
native-generation systems exhibit a moderate positive correlation
($r\!=\!0.266$ for Nontrained, $r\!=\!0.279$ for Trained). For
Nontrained\,+\,MT this correlation is $r\!=\!0.053$.
